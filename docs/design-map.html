Design map · v0.1 · Sept 2026

Ghost needs a stage,
not a world.

You wake dead, somewhere, sometime. Someone alive is stuck. The game is figuring out what kind of ghost this moment needs — and being it.

00 · THESISSituation first, terrain second

Ad Nauseam proves the world can be endless. But Ghost isn't about the world; it's about one household, one rut, one hinge moment. So the generator should be inverted: generate the situation, then grow a setting that serves it, then drop that setting into the infinite terrain. The terrain is the horizon you see out the window. The story is the house.

The Quantum Leap frame gives us a clean loop and a built-in answer to "why is it random every time": you don't choose where you land. Each leap is a fresh seed; the ghost's own story is the only thing that carries over.

WakeNothing known. Era, place, and who lives here all have to be read from the room.
ObserveFollow routines. Notice what repeats, what's avoided, what's broken.
DiscoverClues in objects, letters, talk, and echoes of past events.
DiagnoseName the rut and the hinge: when, who, and what would help.
HauntIntervene with a limited, physical toolkit — timing is the skill.
ReleaseThe pattern breaks. Watch the new course begin. Then you're gone.

01 · GENERATION STACKEight layers, story in the middle

Everything derives from the leap seed, so nothing is stored except what the player changed. Layers are generated top-down at leap time; L1–L2 stream lazily like terrain does now.

Era & placeEra (1870s homestead, 1920s rooming house, 1950s subdivision, 1980s apartment block, 2020s, unplaced future), climate/biome from terrain, settlement scale (lone farm → village → town → city block). Era selects a content pack: architecture grammar, object set, names, idiom, technology, social norms.
SettlementRoads and lots by grammar (grid, organic, ribbon along a road). Buildings by era-specific shape grammar: footprint, stories, roof, materials, wear. Landmarks that read at a glance: church, diner, mill, station.
Interiors & objectsRooms by function, furniture by era, and — the important part — objects with affordances: can fall, can make noise, can be read, can be moved, is fragile, is sentimental. The cup in the cupboard is an affordance, not decoration.
Household & peopleNames by era/region, ages, relationships, jobs, traits, wants and fears. Each person has a routine (schedule by hour) and a pattern — the rut. Pets have routines too, and they can sense you.
The hingeThe dramatic core: a tension (grief, an unmade decision, an estrangement, a secret, a danger ahead, a missed chance), a hinge moment on the clock (the train they'll miss, the letter they won't open, the argument that repeats at 6pm), the nudge that would change it, and both outcomes.
Clue graphClues planted across objects, routines, dialogue, and echoes (replayable past moments only a ghost can see). Every clue is tagged by what it reveals — who, what's wrong, when, what would help — and how subtle it is. Generator guarantees at least two independent paths to a correct diagnosis.
SimulationPeople run routines; anomalies go through notice → attribute → respond. A dropped cup can be dismissed, blamed on the cat, feared, or taken as a sign — which depends on the person's traits and how much you've already done. This is where "any kind of ghost" lives.
Text & voiceLetters, diaries, radio, newspapers, dialogue. Era-correct. Generated from grammars over the situation data so text always agrees with the world. (Biggest open decision — see §05.)

02 · THE GHOST'S TOOLKITVaried in function, like anyone alive

Abilities are physical and cheap to simulate. Depth comes from timing and from how the living interpret them, not from a skill tree.

ChannelWhat you can doWhat it's for
KineticNudge, tip, drop, roll, open/close, knockInterrupt a routine at the right second; put an object in someone's path
AmbientCold spot, draft, flicker, hum, a smell from the pastSet mood; make someone linger or leave a room
PresenceReflection, shadow, dream visit, a shape in the doorwayThe scary route — sometimes exactly what's needed, always costly
AnimalPets see you; lead a dog, spook a cat, wake a birdThe living trust animals; use them as your hands
EchoReplay a past moment in a roomYour main clue source — you see what happened here
AttentionDraw an eye to a thing: a photo, a drawer, a windowThe gentlest nudge; often the whole solution

One shared resource: presence. It recharges slowly; loud acts spend it fast. Overspending makes people afraid, and fear is a legitimate outcome, not just a failure state — some hinges need someone scared out of the house. The game shouldn't judge the tone; it should judge whether the course changed.

03 · CLUE DESIGNSubtle by default, fair always

Clues come in four registers, and the generator mixes them per leap:

  • Object clues — a packed suitcase, one place set at the table, a calendar with a circled date, a medicine bottle, an unpaid bill.
  • Routine clues — she checks the mailbox three times a day; he avoids the upstairs room; the dog waits at the gate at 5:40.
  • Voice clues — a phone call you only hear one side of, an argument through a wall, a radio bulletin.
  • Echo clues — the ghost-only replay: what happened in this kitchen last Tuesday.

Fairness rule: before a leap ships, the clue graph is walked. If a player can't reach "who / what / when / how" from at least two routes without the same clue, regenerate. A journal in the UI lets the player pin clues and form a hypothesis; the hypothesis is never graded — only the outcome is.

04 · LOOK"16- or 32-bit-ish" — three honest readings

Block size is not the thing that will make Ghost feel richer. Legibility of faces, objects, and rooms is. Three directions, each with a real cost:

DirectionWhat it isCost
Sub-voxelKeep the pipeline, shrink the voxel to 0.25 m for buildings, props, and people (MagicaVoxel look). Terrain stays 1 m.Cheap. Mixed-resolution meshing is a known trick. Characters look like toys, which suits a ghost story more than you'd think.
Low-poly texturedPSX/N64 "32-bit": modeled props and characters, affine wobble, low-res textures.Expensive. Needs an asset library; infinite generation becomes kit-bashing, and kit-bashing shows.
Isometric 2.5DSNES/Genesis "16-bit": pixel art rooms, sprite people, the whole house on one screen.Best for reading a household at a glance. Loses the first-person "drift through the wall" feeling, which is the ghost's best trick.
Recommendation
Sub-voxel, first person, with an era palette and a soft day/night light. It keeps everything Ad Nauseam already does, gets you real furniture and readable people within weeks, and the toy-scale bodies make the small interventions feel tender rather than grim. If it fails to charm on a test house, the interior data model carries over to isometric unchanged.

05 · THE BIG FORKWhere the words come from

Everything else in this map is buildable offline in the browser. Story text is the one place that isn't obviously so. Three ways to go:

Grammar onlyTracery-style templates over the situation data. Fully offline, deterministic, always consistent with the world. Variety is finite and will show after a dozen leaps. Cheapest and safest first step.
Pre-baked packsUse an LLM at authoring time to write thousands of era-tagged fragments (letters, arguments, diary pages) with slots the grammar fills. Ships as data. Feels hand-written, still offline, still deterministic.
Runtime LLMGenerate letters and dialogue on the fly from the live situation. Infinite and reactive; needs an API key or a small server, costs money per leap, and consistency has to be enforced. Best kept optional, layered on top of the first two.

06 · ARCHITECTUREWhat the code looks like

Determinism is the whole trick: every layer is a pure function of (leap seed, player actions). That's what lets the world be infinite, saves stay tiny, and a bug be reproduced from a seed string.

07 · VERTICAL SLICEOne house, one hinge

Before any breadth: a single era (I'd pick the 1950s small town — rich object language, strong routines, phones but no screens), one generated house, one household of two or three plus a pet, one hinge, one full loop from wake to release. Sub-voxel look. Grammar-only text. If that thirty-minute experience isn't good, more eras won't save it; if it is, every layer above is just more data.

Rough order: interior + objects → people + routines → ghost kinetic/attention → one hand-authored hinge → clue graph + fairness walk → then make the hinge generated.

08 · OPEN QUESTIONSDecisions that change the build

Camera.First-person ghost, as now? It's the strongest argument for staying 3D. Assuming yes unless you say otherwise.
Words.Grammar-only to start, pre-baked packs when it works, runtime LLM as an optional layer later? This is the fork I most want your call on.
Clock and failure.Does the world run on real hours with a hinge deadline, and can a haunting go wrong — push someone onto a worse course? I'd say yes to both; failure is what makes subtlety matter.
The ghost's own story.Does something carry across leaps — who you were, why you're here — accreting as a meta-mystery? Cheap to add, big for retention. Your call on whether it's in the slice.
Ad Nauseam is the terrain layer of this plan and stays as-is. Next build after your answers: the interior/object module and a hand-authored 1950s house to stand in.
